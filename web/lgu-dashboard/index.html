        Plan a week of meals

        For school and community feeding programs, at this month's prices.

        Each fish, meat, egg or bean dish is used once a week when there are other choices, so the menu rotates.

        Ingredients for the week

        Supplier order, as bought (with bones and peels).

        What it does

        SAPAT links three public datasets: what food costs (PSA retail prices, monthly, by region, refined by province or city and by NCR palengke from DA price monitoring where available), what each food contains (DOST-FNRI PhilFCT) and what each person needs (PDRI 2015). A linear program then finds the basket that meets each member's needs at the lowest cost, or does the most with a fixed budget.

        What each feature answers

        The cost of a healthy day

        • For each province and month, SAPAT finds the cheapest basket that meets a reference family of five's needs for energy, protein, iron, vitamin A and calcium (PDRI 2015), and divides it per person per day.
        • Province prices replace regional ones where PSA surveyed that food in the province; otherwise the regional price is used (shown faded on the map).
        • Watch means the cost is at least 5% above the average of the 3 months before. Alert means at least 10% above that average, or at least 15% above the same month last year.

        How the plan is chosen

        • Each member gets their own portions: energy, protein, iron, vitamin A and calcium from the PDRI for their age and sex, with extra for pregnancy and breastfeeding.
        • Rules keep meals familiar: rice and corn give at most 65% of energy, at least 35% of protein comes from fish, meat, eggs or beans, vegetables fill a third of the plate, little sugar, and no single food in amounts people would not eat.
        • With a short budget, gaps are spread out but children under 5 and pregnant or breastfeeding mothers are protected first, and energy is protected before vitamins.

        Limits

        • PSA lists no milk, dilis, malunggay or tokwa, so calcium is the hardest and costliest need to meet. Iron for women 13–49 and in pregnancy needs iron-rich or fortified foods or supplements, as the PDRI itself notes.
        • A regional price is carried forward up to 6 months when PSA did not survey it. Official prices stop at the province and, in NCR, at about 20–30 monitored markets; barangay and sari-sari prices are not published.
        • SAPAT is meal-planning guidance, not medical advice. People with health conditions should follow their doctor or nutritionist-dietitian.
